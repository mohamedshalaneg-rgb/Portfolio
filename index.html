01 / ABOUT
Who I Am

Structure, before software.

I work at the point where a business problem is still messy — before anyone has agreed what "done" looks like. My job is to sit with that mess: ask the questions no one has asked yet, find the gap between what a process document says and what actually happens, and turn it into something a development team can build against with confidence.

Most of my hands-on experience comes from a national-scale government digital transformation program — a visa platform used across a country's ministry and its embassies abroad. There, "requirements" weren't abstract: a missed exception case meant a real person's application got stuck with no defined next step. That kind of stakes taught me to write requirements like someone will actually depend on them, because someone will.

I'm a Business Analyst by practice and building toward Product Ownership by direction — I care as much about why a requirement exists as about documenting it correctly, and I'm drawn to the parts of the job that sit closer to the product decision, not just the specification.

03 / APPROACH
How I Work

From an unclear ask to a delivered answer.

The path a requirement actually travels — stage by stage, on the work I've done.

01

Business Problem

Start with the real-world gap — not the assumed solution. On the visa platform, this meant asking what happens when a standard process breaks: a failed biometric scan, a denied-party match, a request stuck mid-flow.

02

Stakeholders

Work with a distributed BA team and cross-functional stakeholders to understand who is affected — ministry staff, embassy personnel, and the applicant on the other end of a notification.

03

Requirements Review

Review and validate Software Requirements Specifications each sprint — reading past what's written to find what's missing, undocumented, or assumed.

04

Gap Analysis

Cross-reference documentation against related modules and teams to surface issues before they reach development — including a case where a dependency pointed to a document that didn't exist yet.

05

Process Modeling

Map dependencies and workflows visually (BPMN, Draw.io, ARIS Express) so handoff sequencing between teams is unambiguous, not assumed.

06

User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

Translate the business rule into a precise, testable requirement engineering can actually build — done for seven exception-handling use cases, including a biometric-enrollment-failure scenario.

07

Validation

Check delivered functionality against the acceptance criteria that were defined up front — closing the loop rather than assuming intent survived the build.

08

Delivery Handoff

Work directly with developers, QA, and UX inside two-week Agile sprints so the requirement stays traceable from specification to what actually ships.
